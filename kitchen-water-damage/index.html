Kitchen Water Damage Help in Saint Petersburg

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. For homeowners in Saint Petersburg, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

Signs to Look For

  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • The bottom edges of cabinet side panels swelling or flaking apart
  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • Downstairs, a brownish mark on the ceiling beneath the kitchen

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First hour

Water Runs Under

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Day 3 to 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Sink Drain Leaks

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

Under the Base Cabinets

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

Refrigerator Water Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Shut Off the Leak

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry in Place

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.
