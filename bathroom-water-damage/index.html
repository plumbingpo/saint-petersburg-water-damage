Bathroom Water Damage Help in Saint Petersburg

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. In Saint Petersburg, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

What It Looks Like

  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • The ceiling beneath the bathroom shows a brownish ring or has started to droop
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • Edges of the vinyl floor peeling up, or dark discoloration showing through it
  • Peeling paint or swollen baseboard on the wall next to the shower
How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Pinpoint the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Trapped Steam

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

Leaking Shower Pan

Every use of the shower sends water beneath its floor when the pan or liner is cracked. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

The Room Below

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Light fixtures and wiring can be in its path.
