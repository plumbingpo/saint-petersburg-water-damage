Wet Carpet Drying: What Saint Petersburg Homeowners Should Know

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. For Saint Petersburg homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

Signs to Look For

  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • Dark, rusty lines along the wall where the tack strip has gotten wet
  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
  • The room smells sour and stale, especially after it has been closed for a while
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

After a week

Past Saving

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Soaked Pad Underneath

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Furniture Marks

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

Mold on the Backing

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

How We Fix It

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.
