How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Saint Petersburg?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Saint Petersburg?

The Short Answer

In Saint Petersburg, the usual water damage bill lands in a range of $1,500 to $10,000 or more. Small clean-water jobs can come in under about $1,500. Major flooding or a sewage backup that needs rebuilding can run well above $10,000.

These are typical national ranges, not a quote. Pricing in Saint Petersburg, FL depends on local wages and the going rate for materials, so your number may differ. The only way to get an exact figure is an on-site inspection with moisture readings.

How Water Type Affects Cost

Water type is normally the starting point when a restoration job is priced. There are three categories in the industry standard, and each reflects a level of contamination. As contamination rises, so does the need for cleaning, tear-out of materials and protective equipment.

Across the US, cleanup tends to run roughly $3 to $7.50 per square foot depending on category. Typical figures look like this.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1, clean waterWater heater tanks, faucets and supply linesRoughly $3 to $4
Category 2 (gray water)Failed sump pumps, discharge from a dishwasher or washing machineRoughly $4 to $6.50
Category 3 (black water)Storm surge, flooding rivers, sewageAbout $7 to $7.50

A loss does not always stay in one category. Leave clean water standing a couple of days and bacteria grow, so it gets handled as black or gray water. It is part of why delay makes a job more expensive.

What Affects the Cost Most

An identical pipe break in two houses can lead to bills that are far apart. When a job is priced, the contractor considers the following.

  • Area affected. A larger wet area takes extra crew hours, additional machines and a longer stay.
  • Water category. Clean water is the cheapest to handle. The highest prices come with sewage.
  • How long it sat. Water spreads into subfloors and wall cavities over time. A leak found in an hour costs far less than one found after a weekend away.
  • Materials. Drying goes smoothly on tile and on concrete. Insulation, drywall, carpet pad and hardwood frequently have to be replaced or dried with special methods.
  • Mold. If growth has started, mold remediation is added to the scope, with containment and air filtration.
  • Reconstruction. One part of the bill is drying. Putting back cabinets, paint, flooring and drywall is billed separately, and for a big loss that part is frequently larger.

Estimates usually cover a pair of phases, extraction of the water and then drying of the structure. Plan on 3 to 5 days for the drying in most cases.

Many companies charge for each day the air movers and dehumidifiers run. Three days of drying is cheaper than six.

Price Ranges by Type of Job

Below are the jobs our team handles most in Saint Petersburg, with the national range that is typical for each. Use the numbers to get oriented and not as a firm price.

  • Burst pipe. Drying and cleanup frequently total $1,500 to $5,000, and the burst pipe repair is on top of that. The total climbs when water flowed for hours or reached a second floor.
  • Appliance leak. A washing machine, dishwasher or water heater leak caught early is often $1,000 to $4,000. Slow leaks under cabinets cost more because of hidden damage.
  • Basement flood. The usual range is $2,000 to $10,000 or more, varying with the water's source, its depth and any finished surfaces. Basement water removal in a finished basement sits at the high end.
  • Sewage backup. Many jobs cost $3,000 to $10,000, some more. Sewage cleanup is Category 3 work, so porous materials are removed and everything is disinfected.
  • Roof leak. Often $1,000 to $5,000 for drying and repairing ceilings, insulation and walls, not counting the roof fix.

Will Insurance Pay for Water Damage in Saint Petersburg?

It depends on the cause and on your policy. Sudden, accidental damage, like a supply line failing without warning or a burst pipe, is often covered by a standard homeowners policy.

Gradual leaks are usually not covered. Months of dripping from a fitting may be labeled a maintenance issue by the insurer. Flood water from outside, such as a rising river or storm surge, is usually excluded unless you carry separate flood insurance. Sewer backup often needs its own add-on.

Before assuming anything, read the policy or phone your agent. The deductible is a factor as well. If the work is minor, its cost may be below your deductible and there may be little point in a claim.

Ways to Lower the Cost

What happens early, within the first couple of days, determines the bulk of a water damage bill. You can change the outcome with a handful of easy steps.

  • Act fast. Water keeps spreading until it is removed. Every hour adds wet material.
  • Shut off the water. Learn the location of the main valve ahead of time, and turn it off the moment a leak shows up.
  • Document everything. Shoot video and pictures first, while everything is still in place and nothing has been tossed. Hold on to all receipts.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. It can take as little as 24 to 48 hours for mold to start. After that point, expect added time and added cost.
  • Move what you can. Furniture, rugs and boxes should come up off wet flooring, provided that is safe.

If the power has not been cut, never wade into standing water, and avoid any water that could carry sewage. The risk of being shocked or getting sick outweighs anything you might save. If the water came from outside, leave flood cleanup to a crew with the right protective gear.

Turning a Range Into an Estimate

A national range helps with budgeting, yet no house is the average house. Only a moisture meter inspection reveals where the water went and what drying and repairs will be needed.
